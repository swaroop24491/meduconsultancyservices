When an employee doesn't want to join PF or ESI

Some staff ask you not to add them to PF or ESI. Most often, they want more take-home pay. But if the rules cover them, they must join. Here is why staff say no, what the rules say, and how to talk to them about it.

Last updated {{ page.last_modified | date: "%-d %B %Y" }} · Rules checked on {{ site.data.facts.tools_checked }}

Why staff say no

PF (Provident Fund, also called EPF) gives your staff savings and a pension. ESI (Employees' State Insurance) gives your staff medical care, and pay when they can't work because of sickness, injury or maternity.

When staff say no to them, it is usually for a fair reason. For example:

  • They want more take-home pay. Their PF and ESI shares come out of their pay each month. When money is tight, that feels like a loss.
  • They don't know what they get. Nobody has explained PF or ESI to them in simple words.
  • They worry about the steps. A new employee who has never been in PF gets their UAN (their PF account number) with a quick face scan on the UMANG app. Some staff are unsure about this. Working with us? We guide them. Read New staff and UAN: the UMANG face scan, explained for employers.

These worries make sense. Listen to them, and answer them. But the answer can't be to leave them out.

Can they opt out?

No, if the rules cover them. For staff the rules cover, joining is not a choice for you or for them.

First, PF and ESI must cover your business. PF applies to a business with 20 or more staff, in any industry. ESI applies to a business with 10 or more staff, except seasonal factories. Once PF or ESI applies, it stays. Read Do you need to register for PF? and Do you need to register for ESI?

Then, for each employee:

  • PF: staff whose PF wages are {{ epf.wage_ceiling_text }} a month or less must be in PF, if the rules cover them. PF wages are basic pay plus DA (dearness allowance), not the full salary. Other allowances, like a special allowance, may also count as PF wages. Call us to check.
  • ESI: staff whose ESI wages are {{ esi.wage_ceiling_text }} a month or less ({{ esi.wage_ceiling_pwd_text }} for a person with a disability) must be in ESI, if the rules cover them. ESI wages leave out some pay items, such as HRA (house rent allowance) and overtime.

PF wages must be at least half the total pay. If they come to less, the difference is added to PF wages. The same rule applies to ESI wages.

So you can't leave covered staff out, and they can't say no. Staff who are already in PF can't stop because they ask to. For how PF and ESI differ, read PF vs ESI: the differences for employers in one table.

Above {{ epf.wage_ceiling_text }}: the joint written choice

For PF, staff with PF wages above {{ epf.wage_ceiling_text }} are different:

  • Already in PF: they stay in PF, even if their PF wages are above {{ epf.wage_ceiling_text }}. They can't leave because their pay went up.
  • New joiners above {{ epf.wage_ceiling_text }} who were never in PF: they can join PF if you and they both agree in writing.
  • In PF at an old job? Call us to check before you decide.

How to explain the benefits

Start with what the money does. Tell them: "PF gives you savings and a pension. ESI gives you medical care and sick pay."

Be honest about take-home pay. PF and ESI take the employee's share from their pay:

  • PF: the employee pays 12% of their PF wages. You pay 12%, split between their PF savings and the pension (EPS).
  • ESI: most employees pay 0.75% of their ESI wages. You pay 3.25%. Staff on very low daily pay don't pay their share, but you still pay 3.25%.

Their take-home pay goes down by their PF share, and their ESI share if they are in ESI. But their PF share is their own savings, and ESI gives them medical care and sick pay.

A few tips:

  • Talk before the first payslip. Staff take it better when they hear it from you first, not from a smaller payslip.
  • Say it is the rule, not your choice. If the rules cover them, you must add them.
  • Keep it short. Don't try to explain every rule. Share a guide they can read at home, in English or Kannada.
