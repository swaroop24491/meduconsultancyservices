Penalties for Late ESI Payment - Interest and Damages Explained

If you've missed an ESI contribution deadline, ESIC can charge you two separate amounts on top of the arrears itself: interest for every day of delay, and a discretionary "damages" penalty. This guide is for small business owners and HR staff in Karnataka who want to understand exactly how both are calculated, with a worked example showing the actual rupee cost, before ESIC sends its own assessment.

Why the ESI Due Date Matters

ESI contributions for a wage month are due by the 15th of the following month. Miss that date and interest starts accruing immediately. Stay unpaid for longer, and a second penalty, damages, applies as well - both on top of the arrears itself.

Regulation 31-A Interest - The Cost of Every Day You're Late

Regulation 31-A of the ESI (General) Regulations, 1950 is the interest ESIC charges on any contribution paid after its due date. It is:

  • 12% per annum, simple interest - the same rate EPF uses for its own interest, just under a different rule
  • Calculated daily, from the date of default until the date you actually pay
  • Mandatory, with no discretion involved

Regulation 31C / Section 85B Damages - The Penalty on Top of Interest

Regulation 31C, read with Section 85B of the ESI Act, lets ESIC levy an additional, discretionary "damages" penalty for defaulting, using a slab rate based on the total length of the delay:

  • Under 2 months late: 5% per annum
  • 2 to 4 months late: 10% per annum
  • 4 to 6 months late: 15% per annum
  • 6 months or more late: 25% per annum

Unlike EPF, this slab structure has not been revised - the same rates apply to every ESI default, regardless of when it happened. EPF moved to a flat 1%-per-month rule for defaults from 14 June 2024 onward (see our EPF late payment penalty guide if you also handle EPF), but ESI's rules have stayed as they are.

Rates change - verify before you rely on this

Interest rates and damages slabs are set by ESIC and the government, and can be revised. The figures on this page reflect rules in force as of September 2026. Confirm current rates with Medu Consultancy or official ESIC notifications before acting on a real notice.

Worked Example: ₹50,000 in Arrears, 66 Days Late

Say an employer owes ₹50,000 in ESI contributions and pays it 66 days after the due date - a little over two months late, which falls in the "2 to 4 months" slab at 10% per annum.

Worked example: ₹50,000 ESI arrears paid 66 days late
Regulation 31-A Interest Regulation 31C Damages (10% p.a. slab) Total Penalty Total Payable
₹1,085 ₹904 ₹1,989 ₹51,989

Figures are approximate, using a 365-day year and simple proration for the interest and damages calculations.

So a 66-day delay on ₹50,000 in ESI arrears costs roughly ₹1,989 in interest and damages combined - on top of the ₹50,000 itself. Want to check your own figures? Use our ESI late payment penalty calculator for an instant estimate based on your exact arrears and delay period.

What Happens If ESIC Finds This During an Inspection?

Late or missing contributions are one of the most common findings during an ESIC inspection. See our ESI inspection guide to understand what inspectors check and how to prepare, so a routine visit doesn't turn into a larger assessment.

Avoiding These Penalties Going Forward

The cheapest way to deal with ESI interest and damages is to never trigger them:

  • Pay contributions by the 15th of every month, without exception
  • Double-check your contribution math using our ESI contribution calculator before you file
  • File your half-yearly returns on time, separately from tracking your monthly payment deadline
  • Reconcile payroll against contributions paid every month, so a mismatch is caught early
