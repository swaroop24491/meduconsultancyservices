What happens if you pay ESI late?

ESIC can charge interest and damages (a fine) on top of the ESI you owe. Interest is 12% a year. Damages are 5% to 25% a year, depending on how late you are. ESIC decides the final amount.

Updated 2 October 2026 · Rules checked on {{ site.data.facts.tools_checked }}

When ESI is due

ESI for a month is due by the {{ f.due_day }}th of the next month. Pay after that date and it is late.

Interest

Interest is 12% a year on the unpaid amount. It is counted for each day, from the due date until the day you pay. It is charged on every late payment.

Damages

Damages are a fine on top of the interest. ESIC decides whether to charge them. The yearly rate goes up the longer you are late: 5% (under 2 months), 10% (2 to 4 months), 15% (4 to 6 months), 25% (6 months or more).

Example: ₹50,000 paid 66 days late

You owe ₹50,000 of ESI. You pay 66 days late. That is in the "2 to 4 months" band, so damages are 10% a year.

  • Interest: ₹1,085 (12% a year for 66 days)
  • Damages: ₹904 (10% a year for 66 days)
  • Extra to pay: ₹1,989
  • Total you pay: ₹51,989

These are estimates. ESIC decides the final amount.

If an inspection finds it

An inspection can find late payments. You may then have to pay the unpaid amount, interest and damages together. Read what happens in an ESI inspection.

How to avoid it

  • Pay your ESI by the {{ f.due_day }}th every month.
  • Check the amount with our free ESI contribution calculator before you pay.
  • Each month, compare payroll with what you paid, so a missed payment is caught in days, not months.
