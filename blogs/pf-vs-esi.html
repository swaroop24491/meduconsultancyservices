PF vs ESI: the differences for employers in one table

PF and ESI are two different schemes, run by two different government offices. They have different staff limits, wage limits and rates. Here is how they compare, side by side.

Last updated {{ page.last_modified | date: "%-d %B %Y" }} · Rules checked on {{ site.data.facts.tools_checked }}

What each one gives your staff

PF (Provident Fund, also called EPF) gives your staff savings and a pension. Read PF benefits for employees.

ESI (Employees' State Insurance) gives your staff medical care, and pay when they can't work because of sickness, injury or maternity. Read ESI benefits for employees.

You can share these two guides with your staff. Want to know what your business gets from PF and ESI? Read What does your business get from PF and ESI?

When you need both

Look at how many staff your business has.

  • 10 to 19 staff: ESI applies, except in seasonal factories. PF applies once you reach 20.
  • 20 or more staff: PF applies, in any industry. ESI applies too, except in seasonal factories.

Then look at each employee. PF and ESI each have their own wage limit, and PF wages and ESI wages are worked out in different ways. So one employee may need to be in PF, in ESI, or in both. Working with us? We'll check who must be in PF and ESI.
