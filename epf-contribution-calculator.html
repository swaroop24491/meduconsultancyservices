How PF is worked out

The employee pays {{ epf.employee_rate_pct }}% of their PF wages. You take it from their salary. All of it goes to their PF account.

You also pay {{ epf.employer_rate_pct }}%. Part of it goes to the employee's pension, called EPS (the Employees' Pension Scheme): 8.33% of PF wages up to {{ epf.wage_ceiling_text }}. That is at most ₹2,083 a month. The rest of your {{ epf.employer_rate_pct }}% goes to their PF account.

Some businesses the government has named pay 10% instead of 12%. Most businesses pay 12%.

The PF wage limit is {{ epf.wage_ceiling_text }} from {{ epf.wage_ceiling_from }} (it was {{ epf.wage_ceiling_old_text }}). If an employee in PF has PF wages above {{ epf.wage_ceiling_text }}, you pay PF on at least {{ epf.wage_ceiling_text }}. You can pay on the full PF wages if you and the employee both agree.

This calculator always puts 8.33% of PF wages (up to {{ epf.wage_ceiling_text }}) into the pension. For staff aged 58 or more, or new staff with PF wages above {{ epf.wage_ceiling_text }}, this can be different. Call us to check.

PF (also called EPF) is due by the {{ epf.due_day }}th of the next month.

Note: This calculator gives an estimate. The final figure can differ slightly because of rounding. Checked against the rules as of {{ site.data.facts.tools_checked }}.
