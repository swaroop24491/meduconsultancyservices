How the PF rules work

For your business

Your business must register for PF when it has {{ epf.min_employees }} or more employees, in any industry. Count all your staff, including casual staff, workers a contractor sends and staff on high salaries. Don't count apprentices, the owner or partners. Is your business a company? Call us to check how to count directors.

Once PF applies, it stays, even if your staff later drop below {{ epf.min_employees }}.

For each employee

An employee must be in PF when their PF wages are {{ epf.wage_ceiling_text }} a month or less. This limit went up from {{ epf.wage_ceiling_old_text }} on {{ epf.wage_ceiling_from }}. Staff with PF wages above {{ epf.wage_ceiling_old_text }} and up to {{ epf.wage_ceiling_text }} who were left out of PF before must be in PF from {{ epf.wage_ceiling_from }}.

Above {{ epf.wage_ceiling_text }}: an employee who was in PF in an old job usually stays in PF. A new employee who was never in PF can join if you and they both agree in writing. Whether they also get the pension part of PF (EPS) depends on when they joined PF and their PF wages then. International workers usually must be in PF, whatever their salary. Some are left out under an agreement between India and their home country. Call us to check.

Note: This checker is a guide based on the general PF rules. It is not a final answer for your business. Some cases need a closer look, like staff in more than one state, contract workers or apprentices. Call us and we'll check. Rules checked on {{ page.rules_checked | date: "%-d %B %Y" }}.
