How the ESI rules work

For your business

Your business must register for ESI when it has {{ esi.min_employees }} or more employees. This rule is the same in every state. Seasonal factories are not covered.

Count all your staff, as for PF. Don't count apprentices, the owner or partners. Is your business a company? Call us to check how to count directors.

Once ESI applies, it stays, even if your staff later drop below {{ esi.min_employees }}. For some risky kinds of work, ESI can apply even with 1 employee. Call us to check.

For each employee

An employee must be in ESI when their ESI wages are {{ esi.wage_ceiling_text }} a month or less ({{ esi.wage_ceiling_pwd_text }} for a person with a disability).

ESI wages are the month's pay minus HRA (house rent allowance), overtime, bonus, commission and travel allowance. Are these items more than half the pay? Then the extra part is added back.

ESI works in two six-month periods: {{ esi.contribution_periods_text }}. If an employee's pay goes above the limit during a period, they stay in ESI until it ends.

Staff on very low pay

Staff on very low daily pay don't pay their own {{ esi.employee_rate_pct }}% share. You still pay your {{ esi.employer_rate_pct }}%. Call us to check if this applies.

Note: This checker is a guide based on the general ESI rules. It is not a final answer for your business. Some cases need a closer look, like staff in more than one state or contract workers. Call us and we'll check. Checked against the rules as of {{ site.data.facts.tools_checked }}.
