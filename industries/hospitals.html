How PF and ESI apply to your staff

PF (Provident Fund) is savings and a pension for employees. ESI (Employees' State Insurance) gives them medical care. PF applies once you have {{ epf.min_employees }} or more employees. ESI applies once you have {{ esi.min_employees }} or more.

Nurses and support staff

Many nurses, ward staff and helpers earn {{ esi.wage_ceiling_text }} a month or less in ESI wages, so they must be in ESI. ESI wages are the month's pay without some items, like HRA (house rent allowance) and overtime. If those items are more than half the pay, part of them is added back. Shift and night-duty allowances usually count. So ESI wages can change from month to month.

When pay goes above {{ esi.wage_ceiling_text }}

ESI works in two six-month periods: {{ esi.contribution_periods_text }}. If a nurse's pay goes above {{ esi.wage_ceiling_text }} during a period, they stay in ESI until it ends. You keep paying ESI for them until then.

Agency staff

Housekeeping, security and some nursing staff often come from an agency. If the agency doesn't pay their PF and ESI, the hospital can be held responsible. So ask the agency for proof every month: their challan (the PF or ESI payment slip).

Doctors on consultant terms

Some doctors work as consultants, not staff. But if a doctor works like a staff member, EPFO (the PF office) may treat them as one and ask for PF. Call us to check how to count your doctors.

When staff don't want to join

Nurses and ward staff sometimes ask to skip PF or ESI to take home more pay. If they are eligible, they can't opt out.

It helps to tell them what they get. ESI gives them and their family free medical care, and cash help when they are sick or on maternity leave. PF builds savings, a pension and life insurance. We can help you explain this to your staff.
